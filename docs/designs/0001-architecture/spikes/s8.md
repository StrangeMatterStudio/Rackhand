# Spike S8: time-series encoding

**Question (design 17, S8):** the real bytes per value of Gorilla-encoded chunks on recorded data, against the 7.4.1
estimate of about 1.4 bytes (tiers 0 and 1) and 2 bytes (tier 2), and whether 50 machines with 100 series each fit the
5.2 budget of 2 GB with the G1 retention.

**Answer:** not as designed. Plain Gorilla on the recorded values needs 5.3, 3.3 and 4.5 bytes per value. The SQLite
row cost the estimate left out is also larger than assumed. The projection is **5.66 GB**.

Three hub-side changes bring the values to about 1 byte each, better than the estimate:
- store values as whole multiples of a per-metric step;
- stamp samples with their 15 s tick;
- pick the cheapest of three integer coders per chunk.

With these the projection is **2.40 GB**. The 7.4.1 chunk rule closes a tier 1 chunk every hour, so it still misses
the budget unless the chunk sizes, the retention or the budget change. The options are at the end. The trade-off is
the maintainer's call (7.4.1), so T05 is blocked until it's made.

## Method

- **Recording.** `spikes/s8/record.py` read the 7.4.2 Linux metric set from `/proc` and `/sys` every 15 s for 24 hours
  on the WSL host, from 06:28 UTC on 2026-11-19: 66 series and 391 992 values. The plan also asked for a lab VM the
  maintainer names. None was recorded (see Limits).
- **Encoding.** One delta-of-delta timestamp column and a value column per stored field. Every float chunk was decoded
  again and compared bit for bit.
- **Tiers.** Tier 0 is the raw values. Tier 1 is min, max and avg over 1-minute windows. Tier 2 is min, max and avg
  over 1-hour windows, taken from tier 1.
- **Chunks (7.4.1).** A chunk closes at 120 points or when its hour ends: 120 points (30 min) for tier 0 and 60 points
  for tier 1. Taken literally, the rule would give tier 2 one point per chunk. Tier 2 is taken as 120 points instead
  (see Design gaps).
- **SQLite cost, measured.** 300 000 rows were inserted into the `series_chunks` schema (`WITHOUT ROWID`, its two
  indexes) in compaction order. The file grew by **103 bytes per row plus 31.5% of the blob bytes**, because inserts
  spread across the key leave pages partly empty. After a `VACUUM` the cost is 105 bytes a row in all for 200-byte
  blobs. Without a vacuum it's 158, and retention keeps the pages fragmented. A rowid table with a unique index on the
  same key measured 126 to 141 bytes a row.
- **Projection.** 50 machines x 100 series with the 7.4.1 retention: tier 0 for 48 h, tier 1 for 30 d, tier 2 for
  2 y. It adds the measured SQLite cost and the fixed 0.44 GB that 7.4.1 gives for connection records, job output,
  events and the rest.

The encodings measured:

| Encoding | Values | Timestamps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gorilla (design 7.4.1) | f64 as read, XOR-encoded | ms at read time |
| Quantized Gorilla | f64 rounded to a step: 0.01 for percents and load, 0.1 for temperatures, 1 for bytes, counts, rates and seconds | ms at read time |
| Integer | the quantized value as a whole multiple of its step. Each column of each chunk uses the cheapest of delta-of-delta, delta (both with buckets up to 64 bits) and XOR, plus a 2-bit tag | ms at read time |
| `+ grid` | as above | the sample's scheduled 15 s tick |
| `+ KiB` | memory, swap and filesystem byte gauges in whole KiB (their source is kB, so nothing is lost) | as above |

## Results

With G1 retention and the 7.4.1 chunk rule:

| Encoding | Tier 0 B/value | Tier 1 B/value | Tier 2 B/value | SQLite rows | Projected total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Design estimate | 1.4 | 1.4 | 2.0 | not counted | 1.95 GB |
| Gorilla | 5.26 | 3.26 | 4.48 | 1.63 GB | 5.66 GB |
| Quantized Gorilla + grid | 1.93 | 2.01 | 3.59 | 1.24 GB | 4.04 GB |
| Integer | 3.21 | 1.03 | 1.71 | 0.91 GB | 2.65 GB |
| Integer + grid | 1.00 | 0.99 | 1.66 | 0.85 GB | 2.43 GB |
| **Integer + grid + KiB** | **1.00** | **0.98** | **1.62** | **0.85 GB** | **2.40 GB** |

The same integer + grid + KiB encoding, varied by chunk size and retention:

| Chunks (tier 1 / tier 2) | 30 d / 2 y (G1) | 14 d / 2 y | 30 d / 1 y |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 60 points (7.4.1 rule) / 120 | 2.40 GB | 1.76 GB | 2.08 GB |
| 120 points (2 h) / 120 | 2.13 GB | 1.63 GB | 1.81 GB |
| 1 440 points (a day) / 720 (30 days) | **1.82 GB** | 1.46 GB | 1.54 GB |

For comparison, plain Gorilla stays over budget in every cell. Its best result is 3.76 GB, with day chunks and 14 days
of tier 1.

## Findings

- **Read-time timestamps are expensive.** The recorder's samples were 13.5 to 14.0 s apart, with hundreds of ms of
  jitter, so almost every tier 0 delta-of-delta took 9 to 12 bits instead of 1. With each sample stamped at its tick,
  tier 0 drops from 3.21 to 1.00 bytes per value under integer encoding.
- **Raw float noise defeats XOR.** Values such as `0.00029623697575621277` change their low mantissa bits on every
  sample. Rounding to the step a dashboard shows and coding the result as an integer takes tier 1 from 3.26 to 0.98
  bytes per value.
- **Memory and byte rates cost the most.** Under integer + grid + KiB, the tier 1 values that remain are led by disk
  and network rates (`diskio.*_bps` 23%, `net.*_bps` 5%) and memory gauges (`mem.*_bytes` 16%, at about 3.3 bytes per
  value).
- **Row overhead is now the largest lever.** The 7.4.1 estimate counted no row cost. Measured, 4.8 M chunks cost
  0.85 GB, and 3.6 M of them are tier 1's hourly chunks. Larger tier 1 chunks cut the row count and also code better
  (0.79 bytes per value for day chunks).
- **Larger open chunks are rewritten more.** Rollups append to the open tier 1 chunk every minute, and SQLite rewrites
  the row each time. This is estimated, not measured. With day chunks, about 8.5 MB of rows would be rewritten every
  minute at 50 machines (about 12 GB a day before the WAL). With 120-point chunks it would be about 0.8 MB a minute
  (about 1.1 GB a day).
- **Tier 2 is overstated.** 24 hours give only 24 hourly points per series, so each tier 2 chunk carries its headers
  over 72 values instead of 360. At full chunks, tier 2's 1.62 bytes per value would be lower.

## Where the changes land

All three encoding changes can be made at the hub when it writes chunks. Rounding to the step, snapping to the tick
and the integer coders touch T19 (encoding), T25 (compaction) and T27 (rollups), which aren't built yet.
- **Unchanged:** the probe, the wire format (values stay f64 with read-time timestamps) and the finished T16, T22 and
  T23.
- **Steps:** each metric's step comes from the 7.4.2 catalogue. Metrics without one, such as driver metrics, keep the
  f64 coder, which the integer coder already includes.
- **Precision lost:** percents to 0.01, rates to whole units a second, and sample times to the machine's interval tick.

## Design gaps

- **Tier 2 chunk length.** "Closed at 120 points or when its hour ends" gives tier 2 one point per chunk: 88 M rows
  for 50 machines, about 9 GB of overhead alone. The measurement assumes tier 2 chunks of 120 points (5 days) unless an
  option below says otherwise.

## Limits

- **One host.** The recording came from a busy development workstation under WSL. Servers that sit mostly idle
  compress better, and busy hypervisors may compress worse. The plan asked for one more VM that the maintainer names.
  The real lab isn't connected to the run yet, and an idle local lab VM wouldn't represent a server.
- **66 series, not 100.** The projection scales bytes per value to the 100 series of 7.4.1. It doesn't scale the
  series count measured.
- **The 13.5 s interval.** The recorder's loop ran faster than 15 s, so the grid runs place 24 hours of samples over
  about 25 hours. Bytes per value are unaffected.

## Decision needed (maintainer)

7.4.1 says that if the budget can't be met with the G1 retention, the maintainer decides the trade-off and the design
is updated before the code changes. Every option below uses integer + grid + KiB at the hub. Each changes the 7.4.1
data format and the stored precision.

1. **Keep G1 retention, tier 1 chunks of 120 points and tier 2 kept 1 year: 1.81 GB.** Low rewrite cost. Changes
   tier 2 retention (G1).
2. **Keep G1 retention, tier 1 chunks of a day and tier 2 chunks of 30 days: 1.82 GB.** No retention change, but about
   10 times the row rewrites of option 1 (estimated).
3. **Keep G1 retention and the chunk rule, tier 1 kept 14 days: 1.76 GB.** Changes tier 1 retention (G1).
4. **Keep G1 retention, tier 1 chunks of 120 points and the budget raised to 2.2 GB per 50 machines: 2.13 GB.**
   Changes 5.2.
5. **Record a second host for 24 hours first** (a real-lab server), then decide.

# Spike S8: time-series encoding

**Question (design 17, S8):** the real bytes per value of Gorilla-encoded chunks on recorded data, against the 7.4.1
estimate of about 1.4 bytes (tiers 0 and 1) and 2 bytes (tier 2), and whether 50 machines with 100 series each fit the
5.2 budget of 2 GB with the G1 retention.

**Answer:** no. Plain Gorilla on the recorded values needs 5.2, 3.2 and 4.5 bytes per value, and the projection is
**4.23 GB**. The best encoding measured stores whole multiples of a per-metric step as integers and stamps samples with
their 15 s tick. It needs 1.65, 1.60 and 2.66 bytes per value, and the projection is **2.52 GB**, still over budget with
the G1 retention. The budget is met only if retention changes as well, for example tier 1 for 14 days instead of 30
(1.88 GB). That trade-off is the maintainer's call (7.4.1), so T05 is blocked until it is made.

## Method

- **Recording.** `spikes/s8/record.py` read the 7.4.2 Linux metric set from `/proc` and `/sys` every 15 s for 24 hours
  on the WSL host, from 06:28 UTC on 2026-11-19: 66 series and 391 992 values. The plan also asked for a lab VM the
  maintainer names. None was recorded (see Limits).
- **Encoding.** Chunks of 120 points with one delta-of-delta timestamp column and a value column per stored field.
  Every float chunk was decoded again and compared bit for bit.
- **Tiers.** Tier 0 is the raw values. Tier 1 is min, max and avg over 1-minute windows. Tier 2 is min, max and avg
  over 1-hour windows, taken from tier 1.
- **Projection.** 50 machines x 100 series with the 7.4.1 retention: tier 0 for 48 h, tier 1 for 30 d, tier 2 for
  2 y. It adds one SQLite row per closed chunk at about 80 bytes, which the 7.4.1 estimate left out (0.24 GB). It also
  adds the fixed 0.44 GB that 7.4.1 gives for connection records, job output, events and the rest.

The encodings measured:

| Encoding | Values | Timestamps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gorilla (design 7.4.1) | f64 as read, XOR-encoded | ms at read time |
| Quantized Gorilla | f64 rounded to a step: 0.01 for percents and load, 0.1 for temperatures, 1 for bytes, counts, rates and seconds | ms at read time |
| Integer | the quantized value as a whole multiple of its step, delta-of-delta encoded like timestamps | ms at read time |
| `+ grid` | as above | the sample's scheduled 15 s tick |

## Results

Bytes per value:

| Encoding | Tier 0 | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Projected total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Design estimate | 1.4 | 1.4 | 2.0 | 1.95 GB (without row overhead) |
| Gorilla | 5.21 | 3.20 | 4.48 | 4.23 GB |
| Quantized Gorilla | 4.09 | 2.31 | 3.63 | 3.36 GB |
| Integer | 3.81 | 1.63 | 2.70 | 2.66 GB |
| Gorilla + grid | 3.06 | 3.08 | 4.45 | 4.03 GB |
| Quantized Gorilla + grid | 1.93 | 1.94 | 3.59 | 2.99 GB |
| **Integer + grid** | **1.65** | **1.60** | **2.66** | **2.52 GB** |

The breakdown for integer + grid:

| Projection for 50 machines x 100 series | Size |
| --- | --- |
| Tier 0 (57.6 M values) | 0.10 GB |
| Tier 1 (648.0 M values) | 1.04 GB |
| Tier 2 (262.8 M values) | 0.70 GB |
| SQLite rows for 3.01 M chunks at about 80 bytes each | 0.24 GB |
| Connection records, job output, events and the rest (7.4.1) | 0.44 GB |
| **Total** | **2.52 GB** (budget 2 GB) |

Retention options, in GB:

| Tier 1 / tier 2 retention | Gorilla | Quantized Gorilla + grid | Integer + grid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 30 d / 2 y (G1) | 4.23 | 2.99 | 2.52 |
| 21 d / 2 y | - | - | 2.16 |
| 30 d / 1 y | - | - | 2.14 |
| 14 d / 2 y | - | 2.24 | **1.88** |
| 14 d / 1 y | 2.43 | 1.74 | 1.50 |

## Findings

- **Read-time timestamps are expensive.** The recorder's samples were 13.5 to 14.0 s apart, with hundreds of ms of
  jitter, so almost every tier 0 delta-of-delta took 9 to 12 bits instead of 1. Stamping each sample with its scheduled
  tick alone takes tier 0 from 5.21 to 3.06 bytes per value. Tiers 1 and 2 use window starts, so the grid doesn't
  change them.
- **Raw float noise dominates.** Values such as `0.00029623697575621277` change their low mantissa bits on every
  sample, and XOR encoding can't compress that. Rounding to the step a dashboard shows (0.01%, whole bytes) recovers a
  third of the space. Storing the steps as integers recovers more.
- **Tier 1 is most of the total.** It holds 648 M of the 968 M values (three fields for 30 days). Byte counters and
  rates (`diskio.*_bps`, `mem.*_bytes`, `net.*_bps`, `disk.used_bytes`) take about 60% of tier 1's bytes under
  integer encoding.
- **Tier 2 is overstated.** 24 hours give only 24 hourly points per series, so each tier 2 chunk carries its headers
  over 72 values instead of 360. At 120 points, tier 2 would be about 0.35 bytes per value less (about 0.6 GB instead
  of 0.70 GB with integer + grid).
- **The 7.4.1 estimate left out per-chunk rows.** At about 80 bytes per row, 3 M chunks add 0.24 GB.

## Limits

- **One host.** The recording came from a busy development workstation under WSL. Servers that sit mostly idle
  compress better, and busy hypervisors may compress worse. The plan asked for one more VM that the maintainer names.
  The real lab isn't connected to the run yet, and an idle local lab VM wouldn't represent a server.
- **66 series, not 100.** The projection scales bytes per value to the 100 series of 7.4.1. It doesn't scale the
  series count measured.
- **The 13.5 s interval.** The recorder's loop ran faster than 15 s, so the grid runs place 24 hours of samples over
  about 25 hours. Bytes per value are unaffected.

## Decision needed (maintainer)

7.4.1 says that if the budget can't be met with the G1 retention, the maintainer decides the trade-off and the design
is updated before the code changes. The options:

1. **Integer + grid encoding, tier 1 kept 14 days (1.88 GB).** It changes the `series_chunks` data format and the
   stored precision (7.4.1), the tick timestamps (7.4.2) and tier 1 retention (7.4.1, G1).
2. **Integer + grid encoding, the budget raised to 2.5 GB per 50 machines.** G1 retention is kept. It changes 5.2 and
   the encoding.
3. **Integer + grid encoding, tier 2 kept 1 year (2.14 GB).** On its own this misses by 0.14 GB. Together with the
   tier 2 overstatement and idle servers it may fit, but that isn't measured.
4. **Record a second host for 24 hours first** (a real-lab server), then decide.
